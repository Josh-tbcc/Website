Headaches are one of the most common reasons people see a health professional. While there are many different types, a lot of everyday headaches are connected to what’s happening in your neck, shoulders and upper back.

## Tension-type and neck-related headaches

**Tension-type headaches** often feel like a tight band around the head, or pressure across the forehead and temples. They’re commonly linked with stress, poor sleep and long periods of sitting.

**Cervicogenic headaches** start in the neck. They’re often on one side, can come with neck stiffness, and may be triggered by certain neck movements or postures.

## Common triggers

- Long hours at a desk or on a phone
- Stress and clenching your jaw
- Poor sleep or an unsupportive pillow
- Dehydration and skipped meals
- Driving long distances

## Things you can try

1. **Move regularly.** Get up from your desk every 30–45 minutes.
2. **Check your screen height** so you’re not looking down all day.
3. **Drink water** consistently through the day.
4. **Notice your jaw.** If you clench, try resting your tongue on the roof of your mouth with your teeth slightly apart.
5. **Keep a headache diary.** Note when they happen and what you were doing. Patterns are useful.

## When to see a doctor urgently

Seek medical care straight away for a sudden, severe headache, a headache after a head injury, or a headache with fever, a stiff neck, confusion, weakness, vision changes or difficulty speaking.
