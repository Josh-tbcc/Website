Look around any café on the Sunshine Coast and you’ll see it: heads tipped forward, shoulders rounded, eyes on a screen. We do it for hours a day, and our necks notice.

## Why screens are hard on your neck

Your head weighs around 5 kilograms. When it sits balanced over your shoulders, your neck carries that load easily. As your head drifts forward to look down at a phone or lean into a laptop, the muscles at the back of your neck and upper back work much harder to hold it up.

Do that for a few minutes and you won’t notice. Do it for hours a day, every day, and it can contribute to stiffness, aching between the shoulder blades and tension-type headaches.

## Set up your desk in five minutes

- **Screen height:** the top of your screen at about eye level, roughly an arm’s length away.
- **Laptop users:** raise the laptop on a stand or a stack of books and use a separate keyboard and mouse.
- **Chair:** feet flat, hips slightly higher than knees, and your lower back supported.
- **Keyboard and mouse:** close enough that your elbows stay by your sides.

## Phone habits that help

1. Bring the phone up to your eyes rather than your eyes down to the phone.
2. Use voice-to-text for longer messages.
3. Set a reminder to put the phone down and look up every 20 minutes.

## Two quick exercises

**Chin tucks:** sit tall and gently draw your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Hold for 3 seconds and repeat 10 times.

**Doorway chest stretch:** place your forearms on either side of a doorway and lean gently forward until you feel a stretch across your chest. Hold for 20–30 seconds.

Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, pins and needles or dizziness.
