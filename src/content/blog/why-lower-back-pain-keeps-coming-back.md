If you’ve had lower back pain more than once, you’re far from alone. It’s one of the most common reasons people visit a health professional in Australia, and one of the most common reasons people walk through our door in Yandina.

What we hear most often isn’t “my back hurts”. It’s “my back keeps going”. It settles for a few weeks or months, then one day you bend to pick up the washing basket and it’s back.

## Pain is often the last thing to show up

Your spine is designed to adapt. When one area stops moving well, perhaps from long hours sitting, an old injury, lifting habits or stress, the areas around it pick up the slack. That works for a while. Pain tends to appear when your body runs out of room to compensate.

That’s why the moment your back “goes” is often not the cause. It’s the tipping point.

## Why short-term fixes don’t always last

Stretching, heat, massage and rest can all make you feel better, and they’re worth doing. But if the underlying pattern hasn’t changed, the same areas are still under the same load. Once you’re back to normal life, it can build up again.

## What helps break the cycle

- **Find the pattern, not just the sore spot.** We look at how your whole spine and pelvis are moving, not only where it hurts.
- **Measure it.** At our clinic that includes a thermography scan and, if clinically required, in-house X-rays, so we can see what’s going on and track changes over time.
- **Build strength once things settle.** Simple, specific exercises help your body hold its progress.
- **Look at your week.** How you sit, sleep, lift and move day to day matters as much as anything we do in the clinic.

## Simple things you can try today

1. Get up and move every 30–45 minutes if you sit for work.
2. When lifting, bend your knees, keep the load close and avoid twisting.
3. Walk most days. Gentle, regular movement is one of the best things for a sore back.
4. Notice your triggers. Is it after driving, gardening or a bad night’s sleep?

## When to see someone

If your back pain keeps returning, has lasted more than a few weeks, or comes with pain into your leg, it’s worth getting assessed. Seek medical care urgently if you have numbness around the groin, changes to bladder or bowel control, or weakness in your legs.
