Summer holidays often mean long drives: down to Brisbane, out west, or up the coast. Hours in the car are hard on your back and neck, but a few changes make the trip much more comfortable.

## Set up your seat before you leave

- **Seat height:** your hips level with or slightly higher than your knees.
- **Distance:** you should be able to press the pedals fully with a slight bend in your knee.
- **Backrest:** fairly upright, around 100–110 degrees, not reclined.
- **Lower back:** use the lumbar support, or a small rolled towel in the curve of your lower back.
- **Headrest:** the middle of it level with the back of your head, close behind it.
- **Steering wheel:** elbows slightly bent and shoulders relaxed.

## Plan breaks

Stop every 1.5 to 2 hours. Get out, walk around, and do a few gentle movements:

1. Hands on your lower back and gently lean back a few times.
2. Roll your shoulders backwards ten times.
3. Gently turn your head side to side.

## Pack smart

Lifting heavy suitcases in and out of the boot is a common way to tweak your back. Pack lighter bags, keep them close when lifting, and don’t twist while holding them.

## Passengers too

If you’re a passenger and tend to fall asleep, a travel pillow helps keep your neck supported.

## When you get home

If you arrive with pain that doesn’t settle after a few days, or pain that travels into your leg, get it checked.
